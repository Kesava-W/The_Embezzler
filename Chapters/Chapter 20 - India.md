# Chapter Twenty — India

The Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Amendment Bill, 2025, was listed again on the day's List of Business, no longer Item Fourteen, no longer buried in an afternoon slot nobody expected to matter. First item. Nobody in the ruling party had needed to argue for that this time.

The Minister of Home Affairs read the same statement of objects he'd read once before, nearly two years earlier, in almost the same flat voice - stricter registration, tighter audit windows, the account-freeze clause that had once sounded, to half the House, like the opening line of an authoritarian argument and now sounded, to most of it, like paperwork. He used the word *national interest* twice, the way he had the first time, and sat down.

Nobody stood up.

The Opposition benches held their seats a moment longer than the silence strictly required, and when their leader finally rose, it wasn't with a paper in his hand or a chant behind him - just a short statement, read rather than shouted, noting for the record that his party considered the bill's implications for civil society "a matter of serious and continuing concern," and that they would not be party to what he called, without much heat left in the phrase, "a hollow procedural exercise." Then the benches emptied, quietly, filing out past a well of the House nobody crossed that day, because there was nothing left in the room worth crossing it for.

The bill passed on a voice vote a little after noon, the ayes carrying a chamber that had lost most of its no.

There was no scuffle at the barricades that evening, no placards, no satellite van angling for the dome. Whatever crowd might once have gathered outside those gates had, over the two years since Item Fourteen first sat untouched on a List of Business, run out of the thing that had once given it a reason to - NGO after NGO gone quiet, gone cautious, gone home, until the only people left with anything to protest about had mostly stopped being in a position to say so. The bulletins carried it as a fourth story that night, forty seconds, no ticker, a home minister's soundbite about strengthening the country's institutions, and then the anchors moved on to the weather.

No one in the House that day called it a victory, either. There was nothing left to call it. A bill that had once needed defending simply became, on its second reading, a bill that had stopped needing anything at all - Item Fourteen, once, on a List of Business nobody had thought to read past lunch. First item, now, on a day nobody needed to be warned about in advance.
