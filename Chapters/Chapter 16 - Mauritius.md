# Chapter Sixteen — Mauritius

She came back four days later.

Ashwin didn't ask what had changed in the time between. He didn't need to. It was in the way she stood at the door before she'd said a word - the same height, the same coat, nothing about her outwardly different except the one thing that was, which was everything.

She'd been pale since the night of the beating. Two weeks of it, then four more days on top, a colour that had settled into her face the way weather settles into a season and stops being weather at all. It wasn't there anymore.

Neither of them said much. There wasn't much left, by that point, that needed saying out loud - only a decision already made, standing quietly in a doorway, waiting to be lived rather than discussed.

She left a little after six, unhurried, the door closing behind her with none of the care it had carried the last time.

The courier came ten minutes later, the parcel set down against the frame the way it always was, unremarked by anyone, unremarked by the street, unremarked even by the man who opened the door to find it there.
