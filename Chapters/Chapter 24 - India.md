# Chapter Twenty-Four — India

"The beating." Satish said it the way he said most things, level, unhurried, a man reading a line item rather than raising a wound. "It was flagged as a risk at the time - a variable nobody in Delhi could account for once it started. I want to understand what actually happened that night. Not what the hospital report said happened."

"It was deliberate."

Satish didn't react to that beyond the smallest pause, the kind that meant he was recalculating rather than reacting. "Deliberate how far. That he beat you, or that you let him?"

"That I let him. That I made sure, once it started, that it went exactly as far as it needed to." Ashwin said it without flinching from the word, though something in his jaw suggested it cost him more to say plainly than it had to actually live through. "I'd already decided, before that night, that the relationship had to end - not because I wanted it to, because the operation needed her at arm's length before the frame closed. When I told her to reconsider us, that was the plan arriving early, forced by her parents' ambush rather than on my own schedule. But once Aadhunik was in that room, I understood something faster than I could have planned for. If I fought back, even a little, I stop being the harmless, broken boyfriend and become something worth a second look - a man who knows how to defend himself is a man somebody starts asking questions about. If I take it, all of it, I stay exactly what everyone in that room already believed I was. Weak. No threat. Nothing anyone needed to look at twice."

"And Mrudula."

"Watching him beat me half to death did more to convince her to say the words he wanted than anything I could have argued for on my own." Something in Ashwin's voice thinned, briefly, before he brought it back under control. "I'm not proud of using that. I'm telling you it worked, and that I understood, in the room, in real time, that it was working, and I let it keep working rather than find some other way through."

"You'd finished the frame-up work that same night."

"Hours earlier. I'd just come up from the den - the last of the wiring, the piece that would eventually give the location away when I needed it to." Ashwin said it flatly, an inventory rather than a boast. "Staying the beaten, harmless boy that night wasn't only about her. It was the best possible cover for a man who'd spent the evening finishing the exact thing that would put her almost-fiancé in prison. Nobody suspects the one crying on the floor."

Satish let that sit for a moment. "Two operations closing in the same room, on the same night. That's not something I'd have planned, if I'm honest with you."

"Neither would I have, if you'd asked me in advance. It was luck as much as design that it landed the way it did."

*

Inside it, at the time, there had been no room for any of what he'd just said out loud.

The first blow had cost him a half-second of real shock - not pain exactly, more the pure animal surprise of a body absorbing force it hadn't braced for - and after that, something in him had simply gone quiet and businesslike in a way he would only be able to name properly months later, at a dhaba table, to a man who already knew the shape of it before he said it. Aadhunik's fist connecting with his jaw. *Tunnel's sealed, nobody finds it before I need them to.* A boot into his ribs that emptied a breath out of him he didn't get back for several seconds. *She's watching. Let her watch. This is the version of tonight she has to remember.* Somewhere in the middle of it, floor tilting, one eye already losing its usefulness, a single clear thought arrived and sat there with a calm that had nothing to do with anything happening to his body: *if I raise a hand, even once, all of this was for nothing.*

He didn't raise a hand.

What he did instead, between blows, in the fractions of a second his mind was somehow still free to use, was watch Mrudula's face over Aadhunik's shoulder - not to perform anything for her, not consciously, but because some part of him that the calculation hadn't fully colonised needed, urgently, to know exactly how much of this she was carrying. She was closer to the door than to him. Her hands were at her mouth. She looked, in the half-second his one working eye could hold on her, like a person watching something break that she didn't yet understand she was about to be asked to help break further.

That was the only moment in the whole beating that cost him something the plan hadn't accounted for. A flicker, gone almost before it registered, indistinguishable from everything else on a face already swelling past readability - not calculation, for once, just a plain, unplanned ache at what her face was doing, filed away and set aside because there wasn't room, that night, for it to be anything more than that.

Then Aadhunik's voice above him, flat with rage-turned-leverage, and the calculation came back online in time to hear exactly what was being offered, and exactly what it would cost her to accept it.

*

"You watched her the whole time," Satish said, back at the table, not a question so much as a confirmation of something he'd already half-suspected from how carefully Ashwin had described everything except that.

"I watched her the whole time." Ashwin didn't elaborate further, and Satish, for once, didn't ask him to. He simply turned his own cup a quarter turn, the same small gesture Ashwin had picked up somewhere along the way without either of them ever remarking on it, and let the silence do whatever work was left to do.
