# Chapter Twenty-Five — India

Satish didn't say anything for a moment after Ashwin finished with the location slip, the tunnel, the last of the operational architecture laid out clean on the table between them. He turned his cup a quarter turn, the habit Ashwin had stopped bothering to ignore weeks ago, and when he spoke again, something in the register had shifted - lower, slower, a man setting down one kind of conversation before picking up a different one entirely. Even the tea-stall boy, drifting past to check on them, seemed to sense something had changed and didn't linger the way he usually did.

"I want to ask you something," Satish said, "and I want you to know before I do that this isn't going in any file."

Ashwin waited.

"Mrudula." Just the name, at first, like Satish needed to hear how it sat in the air between them before he built anything further on top of it. "Walk me through what actually happened. Not the operation. Her."

Ashwin had talked, over four evenings now, about a bungalow full of dead men, about a beating he'd let happen to himself on purpose, about a tunnel his own patience had reopened beneath another man's floor - all of it delivered plainly, professionally, the way a man delivers things he's already made peace with saying out loud. This was the first question all week that cost him a visible pause before he could start. He turned his own cup once, an unconscious echo of Satish's habit, and didn't seem to notice he'd done it.

"She was never meant to come with me." He said it looking at the table rather than at Satish. "That was never RAW's instruction, to be clear - nobody in Delhi ever told me to leave her behind. That was mine. My own reading of what the assignment actually required, once you'd told me to use the relationship for access. I decided, on my own, somewhere around the time I started that work, that the operation would end, I would leave Mauritius, and she would stay exactly where she was - hurt, probably, for a while, and then eventually not. That was the plan I was carrying the whole time you knew about the beating and the tunnel and everything else. You just didn't know the last part of it yet."

"And then she didn't stay where she was."

"And then she didn't stay where she was." Something in Ashwin's voice thinned in a way it hadn't for the tunnel, hadn't for the bungalow, hadn't even fully for the beating. "She came back after the breakup and told me she'd rather lose everything she'd ever known in Mauritius than lose me, and I told her to think about it properly first, and she thought about it properly, and she came back a second time having decided nothing about the answer had changed. I had an exit plan built for one person by the time she did that. I want you to understand how close I came to using it exactly as built."

*

The itinerary had sat open on his laptop for three nights before that one - flight out under a name that wasn't quite his own, a route deliberately unremarkable, one passenger, no return date because a man who isn't coming back doesn't need one. He'd built it the way he built everything, methodically, each leg checked twice against the version of the operation's closing days that had her nowhere in it. He told himself, each of those three nights, that this was the responsible version. The clean one. The one that cost her a few months of grief and nothing more permanent than that.

She'd already told him, days earlier, wordlessly, standing in his doorway with none of the paleness she'd carried since the beating - he'd understood that visit for exactly what it was, a decision already made and simply being delivered rather than discussed. He still hadn't deleted the file. Some stubborn, disciplined part of him had kept it open anyway, a fallback he told himself he was keeping only for operational prudence, right up until the night itself was almost on him. He caught himself, more than once across those three nights, reading the same departure time over and over without actually processing it, the way a man reads a sentence he already knows he isn't going to act on but hasn't yet found the nerve to delete.

He was looking at it again, past midnight, cursor sitting on a departure date he still hadn't committed to, when the message came in.

*Tonight. I'm ready. Tell me where and when.*

He read it twice. He did not, afterward, remember deciding anything - only that his hand had moved to the itinerary before his mind had finished catching up with what his hand was already doing, the solo flight plan gone from the screen in the time it took to select it and press delete, no hesitation in the motion at all despite every hour of hesitation that had come before it. What replaced it took him eleven minutes to build - two passengers this time, a route that would raise fewer questions for a couple than for a man travelling alone, a version of the plan that had never once existed in any of the department's own paperwork, because it had never been the department's plan to begin with.

He didn't tell Satish, that night or any night since, that some part of him had known, the whole three nights he'd spent staring at a solo itinerary, exactly which version he was actually going to choose if she ever gave him the excuse.

*

"You went off-plan," Satish said, back at the table, and there was nothing in the sentence that sounded like an accusation, which somehow made it land harder than if it had.

"I did."

"I suspected before tonight, for what it's worth." Satish said it without any particular weight, the way he delivered most things that mattered most. "Your closing reports in those last two weeks changed shape - not what they said, what they left out. A man closing an operation cleanly writes a certain way. You'd started writing like a man leaving something unfinished on purpose. I've read enough exit plans to know the difference." A pause. "I didn't ask. There wasn't anything useful asking would have done except make you defend a decision you'd already made."

Ashwin looked at him properly for the first time since he'd started talking about her. "You could have flagged it."

"I could have." Satish's tone didn't shift, but something in it had, all the same. "I didn't. That's not something I do for operatives I don't trust to have made the right call."

"She's a civilian, Ashwin. Unaware of everything, tied now, permanently, to a man the department will never fully stop having an interest in." Satish said it evenly, procedurally, every word of it true and every word of it, underneath, something closer to worry than protocol. "That's not a small liability you've built into your life. I need you to understand that I'm not asking you this as your handler right now."

"Then what are you asking me as."

Satish didn't answer that directly, not with words - only held Ashwin's gaze a moment longer than the conversation strictly required, the closest thing to an answer either of them was going to get out of him that night. Somewhere behind him, the generator coughed once and steadied, and neither man so much as glanced toward it.
